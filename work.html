CASE STUDY 01 / AI-ENABLED ENGINEERING

Who decides, who acts,
and what enforces it?

As AI agents participate in code review and software delivery, the central question is not whether an AI reviewer is accurate in isolation. It's what authority the workflow grants—and what stops an unsafe action.

The problem

Automation can accelerate delivery, but an AI-generated “low risk” verdict may be mistaken for authorization. Human intention, execution identity, policy eligibility, and merge enforcement can diverge.

The approach

Compare assisted review, agent-executed human approval, narrowly scoped automation, and autonomous approval. Examine revision binding, deterministic exclusions, identity permissions, and fail-closed behavior.

What I built

A public instructional playground and a decision simulator that let a visitor alter authority, failure conditions, and technical safeguards to see which conclusions change.

What this demonstrates

A structured method for challenging engineering proposals. It does not prove real-world production control effectiveness, model accuracy, or that an employer deployed the illustrated workflow.

CASE STUDY 02 / COST OF ASSURANCE

What does assurance
actually cost?

Risk and compliance programs account for requirements, exceptions, and testing—but often lack a useful model for the effort, rework, and evidence quality behind those activities.

The problem

Manual evidence collection and disconnected workflows can create expensive activity without a transparent view of operational value. Cost alone is not a measure of risk reduction.

The approach

Model work across collection, review, conflict resolution, and remediation. Separate labor assumptions from verified financial impact, and examine quality and risk alongside efficiency.

What I built

An illustrative interactive dashboard exploring assurance effort, automation, evidence conflicts, AI vendor scenarios, and remediation routing.

What this demonstrates

A way to ask better operational and economic questions. Scenario estimates are hypotheses, not audited savings claims or verified enterprise outcomes.
